# Nurtly – User Personas

Status: Knowledge Base v1  
Confidence: Medium  
Last updated: 2026-05-31

## Purpose

This document describes the initial user personas for Nurtly.

These personas are not final research-validated personas. They are working personas based on project discussions, product direction, competitor analysis, and the intended market.

They should guide MVP decisions, UX writing, onboarding, feature prioritization, and future user research.

## Important Assumption

Nurtly is designed for parents and caregivers, not children.

The child may be the subject of care, routines, notes, activities, or sounds, but the parent is the user.

## Primary Persona 1: The Overwhelmed Parent

### Summary

A parent who feels mentally overloaded by daily childcare, fragmented information, sleep disruption, and constant small decisions.

This persona is likely to benefit strongly from Journal, Home, and Sounds.

### Context

The overwhelmed parent may be dealing with:

- irregular sleep
- childcare routines
- feeding
- illness
- nursery adaptation
- work-life pressure
- emotional fatigue
- scattered notes and reminders

They may not have time or energy to maintain a complex app.

### Goals

- remember what happened during the day
- reduce mental load
- feel more in control
- have a calmer evening routine
- quickly access useful tools
- avoid overthinking small decisions

### Pain Points

- too many things to remember
- unclear routines
- lack of sleep
- feeling guilty for not doing enough
- too many parenting opinions online
- no single place for useful notes
- existing apps feel too complex or clinical

### Product Needs

- fast logging
- simple daily overview
- calming sounds
- minimal onboarding
- reassuring tone
- low cognitive load
- no guilt-based mechanics

### UX Implications

The app should:

- be usable in short sessions
- avoid complex setup
- avoid demanding daily completion
- make core actions obvious
- provide emotional calm through design and copy

## Primary Persona 2: The First-Time Parent

### Summary

A parent experiencing early childhood care for the first time. They want guidance, reassurance, and simple tools without being overwhelmed.

This persona may benefit from Play, Journal, and supportive content.

### Context

The first-time parent may be uncertain about:

- age-appropriate activities
- routines
- what is worth tracking
- how to structure the day
- how to calm the child
- how to interpret everyday changes

They may consume parenting content from many sources and feel overloaded.

### Goals

- know what to do next
- find simple play ideas
- record important observations
- feel reassured
- avoid information overload
- build confidence

### Pain Points

- uncertainty
- too much contradictory advice
- anxiety around doing things wrong
- lack of practical examples
- fear of missing something important

### Product Needs

- curated play ideas
- simple explanations
- calm wording
- practical recommendations
- optional tracking
- privacy-conscious setup

### UX Implications

The app should:

- explain features gently
- avoid expert-heavy or medical language
- avoid making the parent feel judged
- present activities as suggestions, not obligations

## Primary Persona 3: The Busy Working Parent

### Summary

A parent who wants practical support but has limited time and attention.

This persona may benefit from shortcuts, Home, Journal summaries, and quick Play ideas.

### Context

The busy parent may switch between work, childcare, household responsibilities, and personal fatigue.

They need the app to be fast and useful.

### Goals

- quickly log important information
- check what happened today
- find a quick activity
- start a calming sound instantly
- keep things organized without manual effort

### Pain Points

- little time
- context switching
- forgetting details
- not wanting another complicated system
- limited patience for setup

### Product Needs

- speed
- clear navigation
- minimal required input
- reusable routines
- quick access to frequent actions

### UX Implications

The app should:

- support one-handed use where possible
- reduce typing
- use defaults wisely
- keep flows short
- avoid unnecessary screens

## Secondary Persona 4: The Routine Builder

### Summary

A parent who wants to create predictable daily routines, especially around sleep, evening calm, play, and care tracking.

This persona may benefit strongly from Sounds and future routine features.

### Goals

- create repeatable evening routines
- use sounds consistently
- connect activities with daily rhythms
- build a sense of stability

### Pain Points

- inconsistent days
- difficulty calming the environment
- lack of a simple structure
- too many tools across apps

### Product Needs

- timers
- fade-out
- favorite sounds
- routine suggestions
- simple activity categories

## Secondary Persona 5: The Privacy-Conscious Parent

### Summary

A parent who is careful about sharing information about their child.

This persona may be especially sensitive to onboarding, accounts, analytics, and cloud storage.

### Goals

- use helpful tools without oversharing
- avoid unnecessary child data collection
- understand what is stored
- maintain control

### Pain Points

- apps asking for too much child data
- unclear privacy policies
- fear of data misuse
- mandatory account creation
- tracking-heavy products

### Product Needs

- optional child name
- optional date of birth
- clear privacy explanations
- local-first options where possible
- transparent data handling

## Early Adopter Profile

The likely early adopter is a parent who:

- is comfortable using mobile apps
- values calm design
- wants practical help
- may already use apps such as baby trackers, note apps, white noise apps, or parenting content apps
- is open to paying if the product saves time or reduces stress

## User Research Gaps

The following assumptions require validation:

- Which feature creates the strongest first-week retention: Journal, Sounds, or Play?
- Are parents willing to log daily care events manually?
- Which Journal events matter most?
- Do users prefer local-only storage or account-based sync?
- Would users pay for premium sounds, activity packs, insights, or AI features?
- Which market should be prioritized first: Poland or English-speaking users?
- How much personalization is useful before it becomes invasive?

## Persona Usage

When planning features, ask:

- Which persona benefits most?
- Is the feature useful for tired users?
- Does it reduce or increase mental load?
- Does it require data that privacy-conscious parents may reject?
- Can this feature be explained in one sentence?
